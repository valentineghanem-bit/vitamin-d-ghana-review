Background

Ghana's equatorial location (4°–11°N) provides intense year-round UVB irradiance, yet vitamin D deficiency (VDD; 25(OH)D <20 ng/mL) is highly prevalent across Ghanaian clinical and community populations — a documented but poorly understood tropical paradox.

Data Sources

17 studies (k=17, N=4,318) identified via PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science and African Journals Online, database inception to March 2025, following PRISMA 2020.

Ethical Statement

Secondary synthesis of previously published, de-identified aggregate data. All primary studies reported their own ethics approval per the Declaration of Helsinki.

Spatial Distribution

Choropleth map and Moran/Gi* spatial summary are available in the full manuscript (Figure 3, Supplementary Figure S2). Six of 16 regions (Ahafo, Bono East, Central, North East, Oti, Savannah) have no published data.

Discussion / Conclusion

VDD is highly prevalent despite equatorial sunlight, with severe burden among pregnant women and T2DM patients, and study concentration in Ashanti/Volta. Targeted supplementation, costed food-fortification policy, a single reference-standard assay, and surveillance in unrepresented regions are indicated as priorities for policy development.
